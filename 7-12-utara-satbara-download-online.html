7/12 Utara (Satbara) Explained: How to Download It Online and What Every Column Means

The 7/12 extract is the single most important land document in Maharashtra. Here is how to download it free from Mahabhulekh, and how to read every column on it.

If you are buying land anywhere in Maharashtra, the 7/12 extract — the satbara utara — is the document you check first. It tells you who owns the land, how big it is, what is grown on it, and critically, whether there is a loan or dispute attached to it.

It is free, it takes two minutes, and skipping it is how people lose money.

What 7/12 actually means

The name comes from two village land registers. Form VII records ownership and rights. Form XII records crop and cultivation details. The extract you download combines both into one page for a single survey number.

How to download it from Mahabhulekh

  1. Go to the Maharashtra land records portal, bhulekh.mahabhumi.gov.in.
  2. Select your division — for Vidarbh this is Amravati or Nagpur.
  3. Choose 7/12 as the record type.
  4. Select your district, taluka and village from the dropdowns.
  5. Search by survey number / gat number, or by the owner's full name.
  6. View the record on screen. For a legally valid copy with a digital signature, use the Digitally Signed 7/12 service on the same portal, which requires a small fee and a login.
The free view-only copy is fine for your own due diligence. For a bank loan or a court matter, you need the digitally signed version — the free printout is not accepted.

The Other Rights column is where the problems hide

Most disputes trace back to an entry in the Itar Hakk column that the buyer did not read or did not understand. Common entries worth investigating before you pay anything:

  • A bank or cooperative society charge — a loan is outstanding against the land
  • A tenancy entry under the tenancy laws — a tenant may hold protected rights over land they cultivate
  • A minor's name — sale of a minor's share requires court permission
  • A pending court case reference — the title is contested
  • An inam or watan class entry — transfer may need government permission
  • A right of way or easement in favour of a neighbouring plot
An entry in Other Rights is not automatically a deal-breaker. It is a signal to get a lawyer to read the underlying document before you proceed.

7/12 is not proof of ownership by itself

This is the most common misunderstanding in Maharashtra land buying. The 7/12 is a revenue record — it records who the state believes holds the land for revenue purposes. It is strong evidence, but ownership is established by the chain of registered sale deeds.

So your due diligence needs all three: the current 7/12, the mutation register (6D / ferfar) showing how ownership moved, and the registered sale deeds themselves for at least the last 30 years.

What to do if the 7/12 is wrong

Errors — misspelt names, wrong area, an ownership change that never got recorded — are corrected through a mutation application at the talathi office, escalating to the tahsildar if the entry is contested. Correcting a record takes weeks to months, so start the process well before your intended registration date, not after.

For non-agricultural plots

Once land is converted to non-agricultural use, the relevant record shifts to the Property Card, not the 7/12. If you are buying an NA plot in a layout, ask for the Property Card and the NA order — a 7/12 alone tells you the plot's pre-conversion history.

Frequently asked questions

Is downloading the 7/12 extract free?
Viewing and printing the record on Mahabhulekh is free. The digitally signed copy, which banks and courts accept, carries a small fee.
Can I search 7/12 by owner name instead of survey number?
Yes. Mahabhulekh supports a name-based search within a selected village. Names are recorded in Marathi, so spelling variations are common — try more than one spelling.
What is the difference between 7/12 and 8A?
The 7/12 covers one survey number. Form 8A lists all land holdings of one person across the village, which is useful for checking whether a seller holds other parcels.
How often is the online 7/12 updated?
Records are updated as mutations are approved, but there can be a lag between an approved mutation and the online record. Always cross-check with the talathi office before a high-value transaction.
Does a name on the 7/12 mean that person can sell the land?
Not necessarily. Check the Other Rights column, whether the holding is joint, and whether any co-owner or minor's consent is needed. The 7/12 is a starting point, not a clearance.
