About Bloom

Simple, honest calculators for the questions people actually have about cycles and pregnancy - with the reasoning shown and the limits stated.

What we do and do not do

Bloom provides calculators. We compute dates and averages from formulas that are standard in obstetric practice, and we show you how each figure was reached.

We do not diagnose, we do not recommend treatments, and we do not publish medical advice. Where a question needs a doctor, we say so rather than answering it.

Bleeding and fertility are shown as separate layers. The fertile window is fixed relative to ovulation, not to menstruation, so on a short cycle or with a long period the two overlap - and sperm surviving up to five days means intercourse during bleeding can still result in pregnancy. Our cycle wheels therefore draw them as two concentric rings rather than one, so neither can hide the other, and both calculators name the overlapping dates explicitly when they occur. We treat "you cannot get pregnant on your period" as a claim to correct, not a simplification to preserve. Menstruation is likewise part of the early follicular phase rather than a separate hormonal phase; we label it separately only because bleeding is the observable part.

Fetal length and weight figures are population averages from standard growth references. Individual healthy babies vary substantially at every week, and only your own scan measurements mean anything about your pregnancy.

Where these estimates stop being reliable

  • Calendar prediction assumes fairly regular cycles. If yours vary by more than about a week, accuracy drops sharply.
  • A first-trimester dating scan is more accurate than any calendar calculation. If they disagree, follow the scan.
  • Ovulation shifts with stress, illness, travel and weight change - it is not a fixed calendar event.
  • Only about 1 in 20 babies is born on the estimated due date. Term birth spans roughly 37 to 42 weeks.
On using this to avoid pregnancy. Calendar-based fertility awareness is a recognised contraceptive approach, but a weak one: roughly 12-24 pregnancies per 100 women per year with typical use, against about 7 for the pill and under 1 for an IUD. Our safe sex days calculator shows those figures on the page rather than hiding them, and refuses to produce a window when your cycles are too irregular for the method to apply. The ovulation calculator is built for people trying to conceive and uses a narrower window - do not use it in reverse to pick "safe" days.

Privacy

Every calculation runs in your browser using JavaScript. The dates you enter are never transmitted to us or anyone else - there is no server that receives them and no account system.

To save you re-typing, your last inputs are kept in your browser's local storage on your own device. Clearing your browser data deletes them. The period tracker's logged dates work the same way.

If advertising or analytics are added to this site in future, those third parties may set their own cookies to measure page traffic. That would apply to page visits only - never to the health data you type into a calculator, which stays local by design.

Accessibility

Colour is never the only carrier of meaning. Every chart ships a legend, direct labels and a data table, and the categorical palette is validated for colour-vision deficiency against both the light and dark surfaces. Charts are keyboard-focusable and carry text descriptions.
