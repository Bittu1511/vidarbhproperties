15 Documents to Check Before Buying Land in Maharashtra

The complete due-diligence checklist. Every document, why it matters, and the specific red flag to look for on each one.

Buying Guide 13 August 2026 · 10 min read

What this guide covers

  1. Ownership and title
  2. Encumbrance and dues
  3. Land use and approvals
  4. Identity and capacity to sell
  5. Red flags that should stop the deal
  6. The order of operations

Land fraud in Maharashtra is rarely dramatic. It is usually one missing document, one unread column, or one heir who was never asked. This checklist is what a careful buyer works through before any money changes hands.

Work through it in order. If any item cannot be produced, that is your answer.

Ownership and title

  1. 7/12 extract (satbara) — current, showing the seller as occupant. Read the Other Rights column carefully.
  2. 8A extract — the seller's full landholding in that village, useful for spotting undisclosed parcels or disputes.
  3. Mutation register (6D / ferfar) — the chain of how ownership moved to the current holder. Gaps here are serious.
  4. Registered sale deeds for the last 30 years — the actual legal basis of title. Ask your lawyer to trace the chain end to end.
  5. Property Card — for non-agricultural land and urban plots, this replaces the 7/12 as the primary record.

Encumbrance and dues

  1. Encumbrance certificate — from the sub-registrar, showing registered mortgages and charges over a stated period.
  2. Bank NOC — if any loan was ever taken against the land, get written confirmation it is closed and the charge released.
  3. Latest property tax receipt — unpaid tax transfers to you as the new owner.
  4. Electricity and water bill clearance — for built property.

Land use and approvals

  1. NA order — if the land is being sold as non-agricultural, the conversion order from the collector must exist. A seller saying 'NA is in process' means the land is agricultural today.
  2. Layout approval — for a plot in a layout, the sanctioned layout plan from the local planning authority, with your plot number visible on it.
  3. Zone certificate — confirms the land's zoning under the development plan. Land in a green zone, no-development zone or a reserved area cannot be built on.
  4. Building permission / commencement certificate — if you are buying constructed property.

Identity and capacity to sell

  1. Seller's PAN and Aadhaar — matched against the name on the 7/12 exactly.
  2. Legal heir certificate or succession documents — if the recorded owner has died, every heir must join the sale or give written consent.
The most common land dispute in Maharashtra is an heir who was not party to the sale. If the recorded owner has passed away, insist on documented consent from every legal heir — not a verbal assurance that 'the family has agreed'.

Red flags that should stop the deal

Red flagWhat it usually means
Seller only has a photocopy of the deedOriginal may be pledged with a bank, or the title is disputed
Price is well below marketSomething on the title is wrong; the discount is the risk premium
Pressure to pay cash urgentlyClassic pressure tactic to prevent due diligence
Power of attorney sale, no owner presentPOA sales are frequently challenged; verify the POA is registered and still valid
Name on 7/12 differs slightly from seller's IDCould be a spelling variation — or a different person entirely
Land is fenced by someone elseAdverse possession claim or an undisclosed tenant
No physical access road to the plotLandlocked plot; you may need an easement you do not have

The order of operations

  1. Get copies of all documents before paying any token amount.
  2. Have a property lawyer do a title search — expect to pay a few thousand rupees. This is the cheapest insurance in the entire transaction.
  3. Publish a public notice in a local newspaper inviting objections. Many lawyers treat this as standard for land purchases.
  4. Get a licensed surveyor to physically measure and mark boundaries.
  5. Only then pay the token and sign the agreement to sell.
  6. Pay stamp duty, register the sale deed, and immediately apply for mutation in your name.
Registration is not the last step. Until mutation is complete and your name appears on the 7/12 or Property Card, the revenue record still shows the seller.
Once the documents check out, budget the registration cost before you commit.

Frequently asked questions

Do I really need a lawyer to buy land?
For agricultural land or any parcel with a long ownership history, yes. A title search costs a few thousand rupees against a purchase worth lakhs — the asymmetry is not close.
What is an encumbrance certificate and where do I get it?
It is a record from the sub-registrar of all registered transactions and charges on a property over a stated period. Apply at the sub-registrar office covering that property, or via the IGR Maharashtra portal.
Can I buy agricultural land in Maharashtra if I am not a farmer?
Maharashtra restricts agricultural land purchase by non-agriculturists. The rules have specific exemptions and thresholds, and they change — take legal advice on your specific situation before committing.
Is a notarised agreement the same as a registered one?
No. Notarisation is not registration. Only a registered sale deed transfers title in a way that is properly enforceable.
How long does mutation take after registration?
Typically a few weeks to a few months, depending on the talathi office and whether any objection is raised. Apply immediately after registration rather than waiting.
