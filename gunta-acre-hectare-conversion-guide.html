Gunta, Acre, Hectare and Bigha: The Complete Maharashtra Land Measurement Guide

1 Gunta = how many square feet? A plain-English guide to every land unit used in Maharashtra, with exact conversion figures and the mistakes that cost buyers money.

Buying Guide 14 August 2026 · 6 min read

Land in Maharashtra is quoted in at least five different units depending on who you are talking to. A farmer talks in acres and guntas. The 7/12 extract is printed in hectares and ares. A builder quotes square feet. Getting the conversion wrong by one unit can change a deal by lakhs.

Bigha is the dangerous one. It is not standardised — it differs between states and even between districts. Never accept a price quoted in bighas without converting to square feet or guntas in writing.

The one number worth memorising

1 Gunta = 1,089 square feet. From that single figure you can derive almost everything else: 40 guntas make an acre, and a hectare is just under two and a half acres. If you remember nothing else from this page, remember 1,089.

Why the 7/12 uses hectares and the seller uses guntas

Land records were metricated decades ago, so the official extract prints hectare-are-square metre. Village practice never switched. This means a 7/12 showing 0.40.50 (hectare.are.sq m) is the same land a seller describes as roughly 16 guntas — and buyers who do not convert assume they are looking at two different parcels.

Carpet area, built-up area and super built-up area

For flats rather than land, a different trap applies. Three numbers get quoted for the same apartment:

  • Carpet area — the actual usable floor space inside your walls. This is what RERA requires builders to quote.
  • Built-up area — carpet area plus the walls and balcony, typically around 10–15% more.
  • Super built-up area — built-up area plus your share of lobbies, staircases, lifts and amenities. Often 25–40% more than carpet.

A flat advertised at ₹4,000 per sq ft on super built-up is materially more expensive per usable foot than the same rate quoted on carpet area. Always ask which basis a per-square-foot price refers to, and get the carpet area in writing.

Practical checks before you agree a price

  1. Get the area from the 7/12 or Property Card, not from the seller's verbal figure.
  2. Convert every figure into square feet so you are comparing like with like.
  3. For land, commission a physical measurement by a licensed surveyor — recorded area and actual fenced area often differ.
  4. Write the agreed area, in a single named unit, into the agreement itself.
  5. Confirm whether the price is per gunta, per acre or a lump sum for the parcel — and put that in writing too.
A surveyor's measurement typically costs a few thousand rupees. On a land deal worth lakhs, skipping it to save that is the worst trade in the process.

Frequently asked questions

How many square feet is 1 gunta?
1 gunta equals 1,089 square feet, or about 101.17 square metres.
How many guntas make one acre?
40 guntas make one acre. One acre is 43,560 square feet.
How many acres is one hectare?
One hectare is 2.4711 acres, which is about 98.84 guntas — commonly rounded to 100 guntas in conversation, though that rounding costs you about 1% of the area.
Is bigha used in Vidarbh?
It is used informally in parts of Maharashtra but it is not a standard unit and does not appear on the 7/12. Always convert a bigha figure to guntas or square feet before agreeing terms.
What does 0.40.50 mean on a 7/12 extract?
It is hectare.are.square-metre — 0 hectares, 40 ares and 50 square metres, which is 4,050 square metres, or roughly 40 guntas.
