Home Loan Guide for First-Time Buyers in Maharashtra

Eligibility, documents, interest rate types, the charges nobody mentions, and how to decide between a lower EMI and a shorter tenure.

A home loan is usually the largest financial commitment a household makes. The difference between a well-chosen loan and a poorly chosen one, over twenty years, runs into lakhs — and most of that difference is decided in the first week.

How much you can borrow

Two limits apply, and the lower one wins.

  • Loan-to-value: lenders finance a percentage of the property value, so you need a down payment. The permitted percentage steps down as the property value rises.
  • Repayment capacity: your total EMIs, including any existing loans, are capped as a proportion of your net monthly income. This is usually the binding constraint for salaried buyers.
The loan-to-value percentage applies to the property value the bank assesses, not the price you agreed. If the bank's valuation comes in below your purchase price, your down payment goes up.

Fixed, floating, or hybrid

Floating rates move with the lender's benchmark, which is linked to the repo rate for most retail loans. Fixed rates lock your rate for a defined period. In practice most Indian home loans are floating, because fixed rates are quoted at a premium and long-tenure true-fixed products are rare.

The practical question is not fixed versus floating, but whether you can absorb an EMI increase if rates rise. If a two-percentage-point rise would strain your budget, borrow less rather than buying a fixed-rate product.

The charges that are easy to miss

  • Processing fee — a percentage of the loan, often negotiable, sometimes waived during campaigns
  • Legal and technical valuation charges
  • CERSAI registration and documentation charges
  • Property insurance, which some lenders bundle — check whether it is genuinely mandatory
  • Prepayment charges — floating-rate loans to individuals generally cannot carry them, but confirm for your specific product

Tenure: the trade-off that matters most

A longer tenure lowers your EMI and raises your total interest, often dramatically. A shorter tenure does the opposite. The instinct is to take the longest tenure for the comfortable EMI, but the interest cost of that comfort is large.

A better approach: take a tenure you can service comfortably, then prepay whenever you have surplus. Early prepayments hit principal hardest, because the early years of an EMI schedule are almost entirely interest.

Tax benefits

For a self-occupied property, interest paid is deductible under Section 24(b) up to the prescribed annual limit, and principal repayment counts within the Section 80C limit alongside stamp duty paid in the year of purchase. These benefits interact with your choice of tax regime, so run the numbers both ways with a chartered accountant rather than assuming the deduction applies.

Improving your odds before you apply

  1. Check your credit score first and fix any errors — a dispute takes weeks to resolve.
  2. Close small running loans and unused credit cards; they eat into your eligibility disproportionately.
  3. Do not make multiple loan applications in a short window; each one leaves a hard enquiry on your report.
  4. Keep six months of clean, well-documented bank statements before applying.
  5. Get a pre-approval before you commit to a property, so you negotiate knowing what you can actually fund.

Frequently asked questions

What credit score do I need for a home loan?
Most lenders look for a comfortably good score, and the best rates go to the highest bands. A weak score does not always mean rejection, but it usually means a higher rate.
Can I get a home loan to buy agricultural land?
Standard home loans do not cover agricultural land purchase. Some lenders offer separate agricultural land loans on different terms.
Are there prepayment penalties on a home loan?
Floating-rate home loans to individual borrowers generally cannot carry foreclosure charges. Fixed-rate products may. Confirm in your sanction letter.
Is a joint home loan better?
It raises your combined eligibility, and co-borrowers who are also co-owners can each claim tax deductions on their share. Both parties are fully liable for the whole loan, so it is a genuine joint commitment.
Should I take the longest tenure available?
Only if you need the lower EMI. The total interest on a long tenure is substantially higher — take a manageable tenure and prepay when you can.
