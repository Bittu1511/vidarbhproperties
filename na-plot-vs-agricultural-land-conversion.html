NA Plot vs Agricultural Land: What You Can and Cannot Build

Buying agricultural land and planning to build a house? Read this first. How NA conversion works in Maharashtra, what it costs, and how long it takes.

Buying Guide 12 August 2026 · 7 min read

A plot advertised as 'NA' costs considerably more than the agricultural land next to it. Buyers often assume the difference is a technicality they can sort out later. It is not — and 'later' can mean years.

The distinction in one line

Agricultural land may legally be used only for agriculture. Non-agricultural land — NA land — carries a collector's order permitting a different use: residential, commercial, industrial, or a specific mix.

What you cannot do on agricultural land

  • Build a house for regular residence (a farmhouse has narrow, conditional exceptions)
  • Get building permission from the municipal or panchayat authority
  • Get a standard home loan — most banks will not lend for construction on unconverted land
  • Sell it as residential plots to buyers
  • Get a municipal electricity or water connection on a residential tariff
Structures built on unconverted agricultural land can be treated as unauthorised. Regularisation, where possible at all, is slow and expensive.

How NA conversion works

  1. Confirm the zoning first. Land in a green zone, no-development zone, or a reserved area under the development plan cannot be converted at all. This is the step people skip.
  2. Apply to the collector with the 7/12, mutation extracts, a survey map, the zone certificate and a layout plan.
  3. The application is circulated to the town planning department, the local body and the revenue department for their comments.
  4. On approval, pay the conversion charges and the non-agricultural assessment.
  5. Receive the NA order (the sanad), which specifies the permitted use.
  6. Get the record updated — the parcel moves from a 7/12 to a Property Card.

Cost and timeline

Conversion charges are calculated with reference to the Ready Reckoner value and the intended use, so a commercial conversion costs more than residential for the same parcel. Add professional fees for the architect and the layout plan.

Realistically, budget several months, and treat any promise of a few weeks with scepticism. Timelines depend heavily on whether any department raises an objection.

The deemed-NA route

Maharashtra has provisions under which land inside certain planning boundaries and conforming to the development plan zoning can be treated as deemed non-agricultural, avoiding the full conversion process. Whether a specific parcel qualifies depends on its location and zoning, so verify with the local planning authority rather than relying on a seller's claim.

'NA in process' means the land is agricultural today. Price it as agricultural land and make any premium conditional on the order actually being issued.

When agricultural land is still the right buy

If you genuinely intend to farm, or you are buying as a long-term hold in a corridor where development is heading, unconverted land at agricultural prices can be the better investment — provided the zoning permits eventual conversion. The mistake is paying an NA-adjacent price for land that is neither converted nor convertible.

Frequently asked questions

What does NA plot mean?
NA stands for non-agricultural. It is land for which the collector has issued an order permitting a non-agricultural use such as residential or commercial.
Can I build a farmhouse on agricultural land in Maharashtra?
There are narrow, conditional allowances for farmhouse construction on agricultural holdings, subject to size limits and permissions. It is not a general right to build a residence — confirm with the local authority before you buy.
Will a bank give me a home loan on agricultural land?
Generally no for construction finance. Some lenders offer agricultural land loans, but these are different products on different terms.
How long does NA conversion take in Maharashtra?
Typically several months. It depends on zoning, departmental clearances, and whether objections are raised.
Is deemed NA the same as an NA order?
Not identical. Deemed NA arises from statutory provisions for land conforming to the development plan within certain boundaries. Whether it applies to your plot must be confirmed with the planning authority.
