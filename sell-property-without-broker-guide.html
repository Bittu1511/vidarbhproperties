How to Sell Your Property Without a Broker: A Practical Guide

Selling directly saves you the commission but puts the work on you. Here is exactly what to do — pricing, photos, the listing, handling buyers, and closing safely.

Broker commission on a property sale in Maharashtra typically runs to a meaningful percentage of the sale value, often split between buyer and seller. On a ₹25 lakh sale that is real money — and increasingly, sellers are handling the process themselves.

Direct selling is not difficult. It is just work that someone has to do, and if you do it, you keep the fee.

Step 1 — Price it honestly

The most expensive mistake in a direct sale is over-pricing. A property listed 20% above market does not sell slowly; it sits, goes stale, and eventually sells below what a correctly priced listing would have achieved.

  1. Look up the Ready Reckoner value for your survey number — this is your floor reference, not your asking price.
  2. Find at least five comparable listings in the same locality with a similar area and condition.
  3. Adjust for the specific differences: road width, corner plot, floor level, age, water availability.
  4. Set your asking price about 5% above your target so you have negotiating room, but no more.

Step 2 — Get the documents ready first

Serious buyers ask for documents within the first conversation. Having them scanned and ready makes you look credible and speeds up the deal by weeks.

  • Current 7/12 extract or Property Card
  • Registered sale deed showing your ownership
  • Latest property tax receipt
  • NA order and approved layout, if applicable
  • Loan closure NOC, if the property was ever mortgaged
  • Your PAN and Aadhaar

Step 3 — Photograph it properly

Photos are the entire first impression. Listings with clear daylight photos get dramatically more enquiries than listings with dark, cluttered phone shots.

  1. Shoot between 9am and 11am or in the late afternoon — avoid harsh midday shadow.
  2. Clear the frame. Remove vehicles, drying clothes, and clutter before you shoot.
  3. For land, include a wide shot showing the road access and a shot showing the boundary markers.
  4. For a house, shoot every room from the doorway corner — it makes rooms look their actual size.
  5. Take 8–10 photos and use the best 5. The first photo should be the strongest one you have.

Step 4 — Write a listing that answers questions

A weak listing says 'good plot, prime location, contact for details'. A strong listing pre-answers what every buyer will ask: exact area, exact location, road width, water source, NA status, and why you are selling.

Include the area in a single named unit and state whether the price is per gunta or a lump sum. Vague area and vague pricing are the two things that make buyers assume there is a problem.

Step 5 — Handle enquiries efficiently

  • Answer within a few hours. Property enquiries go cold fast.
  • Qualify early: ask whether they are paying cash or taking a loan, and their timeline. This tells you who is real.
  • Group site visits into one or two slots rather than making a separate trip for every caller.
  • Never hand over original documents. Share scans; originals go to the sub-registrar only.

Step 6 — Close safely

  1. Agree the price and terms in writing before taking any token amount.
  2. Take the token by bank transfer or cheque, never cash — you need a traceable record.
  3. Execute an agreement to sell setting out the price, the payment schedule and the completion date.
  4. Let the buyer complete their due diligence. Cooperate with it; obstruction kills deals.
  5. The buyer pays stamp duty; you both attend the sub-registrar with two witnesses to register the sale deed.
  6. Receive the balance payment before or at registration — never after.
  7. Hand over originals only once payment has cleared, and assist with the buyer's mutation application.
Do not accept a large cash component. Beyond the legal limits on cash transactions, you will have money you cannot account for and the buyer will have a deed that under-states the price.

When a broker is genuinely worth it

Direct selling works best when you have time and the property is in a locality with active demand. If you are selling remotely, if the property has a complicated title, or if you simply cannot manage site visits, a good local broker earns their fee. The decision is about your time, not about principle.

Frequently asked questions

Is it legal to sell property without a broker in India?
Yes. There is no requirement to use an agent. The sale deed is executed between buyer and seller and registered at the sub-registrar office.
Who pays stamp duty, the buyer or the seller?
By convention in Maharashtra the buyer pays stamp duty and registration charges, though the parties can agree otherwise in the agreement.
How much token amount should I take?
A token is usually a small percentage of the sale value, enough to show commitment. Take it by traceable payment and document the terms under which it is refundable.
Do I pay capital gains tax when I sell?
Generally yes on a gain, with the treatment depending on how long you held the property. There are reinvestment exemptions available — take advice from a chartered accountant before you close.
Can I list on multiple platforms at once?
Yes, and you should. There is no exclusivity unless you sign an agreement creating it.
