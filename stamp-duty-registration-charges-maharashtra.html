Stamp Duty and Registration Charges in Maharashtra: Complete Guide

What you actually pay when you register a property in Maharashtra — stamp duty, registration fee, metro cess, the 1% women's concession, and how the amount is calculated.

Stamp duty is the single largest add-on cost when you buy property in Maharashtra — usually more than your lawyer, agent and paperwork combined. Most first-time buyers budget for the property price and are then surprised by a bill running into lakhs at the sub-registrar's office.

This guide explains what you pay, how it is calculated, and the concessions that are easy to miss.

What stamp duty actually is

Stamp duty is a state tax on the legal document that transfers property ownership. Until the sale deed is stamped and registered, the transfer is not legally recorded — which means it will not stand up in a dispute and you cannot get a home loan against it. It is not optional and it is not negotiable.

How Maharashtra calculates the amount

This is the part that catches people out: stamp duty is not charged on the price you paid. It is charged on whichever is HIGHER — the agreement value, or the government's Ready Reckoner value for that property.

If you buy a plot for ₹18 lakh but the Ready Reckoner value works out to ₹22 lakh, stamp duty is calculated on ₹22 lakh. Budget for the reckoner value, not your negotiated price.

The components of your bill

ComponentTypically charged onNotes

Metro cessSame baseApplies only in cities with metro projects — Mumbai, Pune, Nagpur, Thane

In practice, a buyer in a municipal corporation area such as Nagpur pays a higher combined rate than a buyer in a gram panchayat area, because LBT and metro cess apply in the city but not in the village.

The 1% concession for women buyers

Maharashtra offers a concession on stamp duty when the property is registered in a woman's name. This is one of the most under-used savings available to buyers — on a ₹40 lakh property it is a meaningful amount for a single change on the deed.

Two conditions matter: the property must be residential, and it must be registered solely in the woman's name (joint registration with a male co-owner generally does not qualify for the full concession). There is also a lock-in period during which resale to a male buyer can trigger recovery of the concession — check the current condition before you plan a quick resale.

Registration fee and its cap

Registration fee is charged as a percentage of the same base value, but it is capped at a maximum amount. This means that on higher-value properties the registration fee stops growing — it is stamp duty, not registration fee, that scales with price.

Worked example

Take a ₹30 lakh flat in a municipal corporation area, with a Ready Reckoner value of ₹32 lakh. Stamp duty is computed on ₹32 lakh, not ₹30 lakh. Add LBT and, if the city has a metro project, metro cess on the same ₹32 lakh. Then add the registration fee, up to its cap. The realistic total lands in the range of 6–7% of the base value — roughly ₹2 lakh on this transaction.

Costs people forget to budget for

  • GST — applies to under-construction property only, not to ready properties with a completion certificate and not to resale
  • Legal fees for title search and deed drafting
  • Society transfer charges, if you are buying in a housing society
  • Home loan processing fee, typically a percentage of the loan amount
  • Mutation charges to get your name into the municipal records after registration

How to actually pay

  1. Calculate the base value — the higher of your agreement value and the Ready Reckoner value.
  2. Pay stamp duty online through GRAS, the Maharashtra government receipt accounting system, and keep the e-challan.
  3. Book a registration appointment on the IGR Maharashtra portal.
  4. Attend the sub-registrar office with both parties, two witnesses, PAN, Aadhaar and the original documents.
  5. Complete biometric verification and collect the registered document.
Never sign an agreement that under-states the price to reduce stamp duty. The department can reassess at the Ready Reckoner value, levy a penalty, and the under-stated portion becomes unaccounted cash you cannot legally prove you paid.

Where to verify current rates

Rates and concessions change in the state budget and via notification. Always confirm the number that applies on your registration date on the official IGR Maharashtra portal (igrmaharashtra.gov.in) or with your sub-registrar office. Treat every rate you read online — including in this guide — as a planning figure, not a final one.

Frequently asked questions

Is stamp duty calculated on the market price or the Ready Reckoner rate?
On whichever is higher. If your agreement value is below the Ready Reckoner value for that location, the department will use the reckoner value.
Can I claim stamp duty as a tax deduction?
Stamp duty and registration charges paid on a residential house can be claimed under Section 80C, but only in the financial year in which they are actually paid, and within the overall 80C limit.
Do I pay stamp duty on agricultural land?
Yes. Agricultural land transfers are stamped and registered too, though the applicable rate and the reckoner valuation basis differ from residential property.
What happens if I do not register the sale deed?
An unregistered sale deed is not admissible as evidence of title in most circumstances. You cannot mortgage the property, you cannot reliably resell it, and you have very weak standing in a dispute.
Is the women's concession available if the property is jointly owned?
The full concession generally requires sole registration in the woman's name. Joint ownership with a male co-owner usually does not qualify — confirm the current rule with your sub-registrar.
