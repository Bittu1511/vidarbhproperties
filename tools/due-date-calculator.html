Frequently asked questions

How is a due date calculated?
The standard method, Naegele's rule, adds 280 days (40 weeks) to the first day of your last menstrual period. This calculator also adjusts for cycle length — if your cycle runs 32 days rather than 28, ovulation happened later and the estimate shifts accordingly.
Why count from my last period when I wasn't pregnant then?
Because it is a date you can actually identify. The exact moment of conception usually isn't known. The consequence is that the first two weeks of a "40-week" pregnancy happen before conception — which is why you are considered "4 weeks pregnant" about two weeks after conception.
How accurate is the estimated due date?
Roughly 1 in 20 babies arrives on the exact date. A normal term birth is anywhere from 37 to 42 weeks. Early ultrasound dating is more accurate than calendar dating, particularly if your cycles are irregular.
How do I calculate a due date after IVF?
From the transfer date, accounting for embryo age. A day-5 blastocyst transfer gives a due date 261 days later; a day-3 embryo transfer gives 263 days. Select the matching option above.
What if my scan gives a different date?
Follow the scan. First-trimester ultrasound measurement is the most reliable dating method, and clinicians will usually re-date the pregnancy if the scan differs from your dates by more than about a week.
