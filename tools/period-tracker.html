Frequently asked questions

What is a normal cycle length?
Most cycles run 21 to 35 days, measured from the first day of one period to the first day of the next. A few days' variation between cycles is entirely normal - perfectly identical cycles are the exception, not the rule.
How many periods should I log?
At least three, ideally six. One cycle tells you almost nothing, because natural variation is wide enough that any single number could be an outlier.
My period is late - does that mean I'm pregnant?
It might, but stress, illness, significant weight change, travel across time zones, heavy exercise and thyroid or hormonal conditions all delay periods. A home pregnancy test from the day of your missed period is reliable.
When should irregular periods be checked?
See a gynaecologist if cycles are consistently under 21 days or over 35, if you skip three or more in a row, if bleeding lasts more than seven days or is unusually heavy, or if you bleed between periods. PCOS and thyroid conditions are common and treatable.
